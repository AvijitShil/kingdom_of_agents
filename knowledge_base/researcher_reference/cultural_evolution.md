# Researcher Reference: Cultural Evolutionary Theory

Cultural evolutionary theory models culture as an ongoing evolutionary process characterized by the transmission, modification, and survival of socially learned information (Mesoudi, 2016; Creanza et al., 2017).

## Six Core Mechanisms in EON 2.0

1. **Variation**: Agents invent novel survival strategies, trading contracts, rituals, and organizational designs.
2. **Transmission**: Information spreads through six distinct social pathways:
   - *Vertical*: Parent to offspring during education.
   - *Horizontal*: Peer-to-peer diffusion within cohorts.
   - *Oblique*: Elders transmitting to unrelated youth.
   - *Prestige-biased*: Imitation of high-reputation, high-status agents.
   - *Success-biased*: Direct imitation of agents with superior wealth or longevity.
   - *Conformist*: Copying the majority behavior when uncertain.
3. **Selection**: Traits that improve agent solvency and metabolic security are disproportionately retained and transmitted.
4. **Retention**: High-utility behaviors crystallize into institutional norms and domestic traditions.
5. **Transformation**: Cultural recombination and cognitive drift modify existing traits across generations.
6. **Extinction**: Strategies that fail to meet survival constraints or lead to ruin disappear with the demise or bankruptcy of their adherents.
